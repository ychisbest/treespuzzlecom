## 1. One hour \(and a half\) of “Wait\.\.\.Ohhh” II

- Reviewer: Eleanora
- Verdict: Recommended

After HER TREES : THE PUZZLE HOUSE, I honestly doubted the dev could pull off 40+ new puzzles without repeating himself...and yet here we are. HER TREES : PUZZLE DREAM more than doubles the count of the first title, and I stand corrected. The concept is still beautifully simple: figure out which letters to click and in what order. The solutions are easy on paper, but spotting them is the real challenge. I clocked about 1h30 and felt both smart and mildly outplayed.

The hint system is again step by step and flexible, but this time hints are timed and the game tracks how many you use at the end. There are no achievements tied to restraint, so it is purely a personal pride thing, but perfectionists might feel watched. I did not use hints, so I cannot comment on recharge behavior, but the timer alone already changes the vibe compared to the previous game. On the plus side, the world guides you more clearly with interaction markers and lights showing where puzzles are, so I was never lost on what was the next step to take.

The puzzle variety is honestly impressive. Even when I thought “I’ve seen this before”, the twist said otherwise. Difficulty, however, is a bit uneven this time. I completed some puzzles in a matter of seconds, while others took me over 20 minutes of intense overthinking. Beginners might breeze through some sections and then hit a wall, while veterans may crave consistency. It's still sensory friendly though, with calm music, no bright lights, no loud sounds, and no global timer breathing down your neck. Levels are grouped into areas, so there is a bit more running around if you skip and revisit puzzles (but nothing too long).

Content wise it is solid for the price, but comparatively higher than the first game time-wise, so the bundle is the smarter move. I would especially recommend it to seasoned puzzlers, even if technically anyone can finish it.

Her Trees: Puzzle Dream is a delightful point-and-click puzzler. It is a great get-away from "busy" gaming and your only task is to follow the cat and solve one conundrum after another.  The game has no voice-overs or text, is presented in black and white, and has a soothing artistic style and soundtrack.  All focus is on the puzzles which have the same core objective (to determine which letters to click on a 3 x 3 grid and in what order). However, each puzzle is unique and they unfold as you explore a fairly simple environment.

As you move through the puzzles, you are provided with different objects and shapes to combine into a solution. Sounds easy? Think again!  The puzzles are not hard but will definitely require some creative visualization and pattern identification. If you are truly stuck, there is a graduated hint system that becomes accessible, over time.  Steam achievements are not supported. While some may see this as a drawback, I felt fully compensated just by discovering the various solutions and completing the game using minimal hints. 

I highly recommend Her Trees: Puzzle Dream to those who enjoy a few hours of simple focus, studying each puzzle without time constraints or competing story elements.  If you enjoy this game, then I'd also recommend taking a peek at Her Trees: Puzzle House which was released in 2024 and has similar mechanics.

Her Trees: Puzzle Dream is a puzzle game, the second of (currently) two such games from this developer. This one is longer than the first game (The Puzzle House) and has a higher price; I bought both games in a bundle with a 10% discount. As with its predecessor, the art style is very minimalist with greyscale sketches and some basic animation, but I quite liked it. Unlike The Puzzle House, this game takes place in different locations including sub-areas within rooms; some other interface and gameplay improvements have been made by the developer, in creditable response to players' comments and suggestions.

As before, the most important thing to bear in mind in this game is that, for each puzzle, the aim is to obtain a sequence of letters to input into a 3x3 grid containing the letters A to I. Some puzzles produce the letters directly (e.g. by rearranging parts of the puzzle until letters appear in the gaps or in the overlapping areas); others result in a pathway pattern which has to followed around the letter grid. Each solved puzzle grows a flower/fruit on an initially bare tree outline; the game ends when all branches have a flower/fruit. The puzzles weren't particularly hard, especially having familiarity with the first game, but there is a good incremental hint system which gives increasingly detailed information about how to tackle a puzzle, or how to completely solve it.

The game data can be reset from the main menu, allowing the game to be replayed from scratch. My play time (3.5 hours) represents one playthrough. Overall I enjoyed this game and thought it was worth its discounted price.

HER TREES : PUZZLE DREAM is a minimalist, dreamlike puzzle experience developed and published by Stone that invites players into a quiet, monochrome world shaped more by intuition than explicit narrative. Rather than overwhelming players with complex systems or dense storytelling, it offers a carefully curated sequence of handcrafted puzzles set within surreal environments that feel like fragments of memory or imagination. The game is brief in length, but its atmosphere and tactile design leave a lasting impression that lingers beyond its modest runtime.

The core of the experience revolves around point-and-click interaction within self-contained puzzle scenes. Players can pick up objects, reposition elements, and combine items in ways that feel organic rather than rigidly rule-bound. There is no traditional HUD clutter or intrusive guidance; instead, each scene encourages observation and experimentation. Solutions often reveal themselves not through brute-force logic but through a gradual understanding of spatial relationships and subtle environmental cues. This design choice gives the game a meditative rhythm. There is rarely a sense of urgency, and the absence of timers allows players to explore without pressure.

What sets HER TREES : PUZZLE DREAM apart is its striking black-and-white presentation. The entire experience unfolds in grayscale, lending it the feel of a hand-drawn sketchbook or ink illustration. This visual restraint enhances the dreamlike tone and focuses attention on shapes, contrast, and movement rather than color-coded hints. The art direction feels intentional and cohesive, with each puzzle vignette resembling a carefully composed piece of visual poetry. The lack of reliance on color also makes the game accessible to players regardless of color perception, reinforcing its universal design philosophy.

Sound design is equally understated. Instead of a sweeping musical score, the game uses minimal ambient effects that quietly complement each environment. The subdued audio landscape supports the contemplative pacing and reinforces the sense that players are wandering through a subconscious realm. The absence of dialogue or overt storytelling pushes players to interpret meaning through imagery and interaction rather than exposition. This ambiguity may feel abstract, but it also allows the experience to resonate differently with each individual.

The puzzles themselves range in complexity. Some are immediately graspable, built around simple object placement or pattern recognition, while others require more lateral thinking. The game strikes a balance between challenge and accessibility by offering optional hints after a short period of struggle. This prevents frustration without undermining the satisfaction of discovery. When solutions click into place, they often feel elegantly simple in hindsight, a hallmark of well-designed puzzle mechanics.

Although the game does not present a linear story in a traditional sense, there is a subtle emotional arc woven through its sequence of dream fragments. Each puzzle feels like a symbolic vignette, hinting at themes of growth, reflection, and inner exploration. The title itself suggests a personal or introspective journey, and the quiet progression from one scene to the next carries a sense of gradual unfolding. Rather than delivering a clear narrative conclusion, the game invites interpretation, encouraging players to find their own meaning in its imagery.

Its relatively short runtime ensures that the experience remains focused and cohesive. There is little filler; each puzzle contributes to the overall tone and progression. However, players seeking extended playtime or complex mechanical depth may find the brevity limiting. The game is best approached as a compact artistic experience rather than a sprawling challenge.

Ultimately, HER TREES : PUZZLE DREAM stands as a thoughtful and atmospheric entry in the indie puzzle genre. It prioritizes mood, visual composition, and intuitive interaction over spectacle or high difficulty. By embracing simplicity and subtlety, it creates a space for quiet engagement and reflective problem-solving. For players who appreciate minimalist design and puzzles that feel like stepping into a living sketchbook, it offers a serene and quietly memorable journey through the landscape of a dream.
